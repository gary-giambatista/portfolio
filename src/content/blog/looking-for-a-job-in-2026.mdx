<h2 id="story"><span class="num">01</span>My story</h2>

While searching for my next role, I’ve always considered myself lucky to be based in the NYC area where there are so many jobs. But my search has been tough and I’m trying to figure out how I can do better. Part of this involves me coming to terms with the fact that I’ve only landed 1 interview in nearly half a year of applying for relevant roles. With pressure on, I prepared rigorously for the interview, but I failed to pass because I couldn’t solve <a href="https://leetcode.com/problems/minimum-number-of-valid-strings-to-form-target-i/">a very difficult Leetcode problem</a>. Unlucky or lack of skill? Regardless, I know I need to keep working hard memorizing however many algorithms as it takes and trying again and again for as long as it takes! Unfortunately, again has not yet come.

I have been wondering why it is so hard to get an interview? Is it me, or is it what I am doing? There are thousands of jobs, right? So, I tried adjusting my strategy to include sending DMs to targeted hiring managers, but so far that has resulted in little more than a lot of unread messages. Nothing seems to be helping me get interviews. What are you guys experiencing? Has anyone found a strategy that is working? The scariest part of all, is that according to Google, on average software engineers will need 20 interviews before landing a job!

<h2 id="resume"><span class="num">02</span>Is it me or my resume?</h2>

I wonder if the reality of my situation is that I am of too little value in the job market to be worth interviewing? At least on paper? Do you guys feel like that too, like the job market doesn’t want you? Recently, I met with a recruiter from an agency focusing on highlighting people beyond their resumes to help with this problem. The boss there told me my resume wasn’t sexy and they wouldn’t be able to show it to their clients, so they strongly emphasized that for someone like me, my only chance is to build a world class portfolio site to gain attention. That confirmed what I was suspecting, my peers have superior resumes than I do and I cannot win that battle for attention. So, I built my site: <a href="https://gary-giambatista.com/">https://gary-giambatista.com/</a>. Will this help get interviews? Please let me know what you think. So far, no improvements and I’ve heard nothing back from the boss after I sent it over.

Actually, I can’t help but doubt that anyone will be impressed enough to give me an interview over a site or anything I build these days. For example, while building my site I sent it over to a friend hoping to hear some encouraging words and he said “Cool, but didn’t you just vibe code it?”, talk about a buzzkill, but this is 2026 and he wasn’t wrong. Claude wrote the code and I directed Claude. We built it as a team, emphasizing each other’s strengths; my vision and Claude’s super human coding skills. What’s wrong with that? Nothing, except for the fact that anyone can build software like this and that means my marketplace value has plummeted. Are my technical skills even worth anything anymore? Did the recruiters realize that before me and they already knew everything on my resume is worthless because it can all be done with a prompt? Maybe that’s why I can’t get interviews?! As much as I have been struggling, maybe this is all my fault for trying to pretend like I have any value as a software engineer in today’s market where AI models can build businesses the software they need?

<h2 id="next"><span class="num">03</span>My next method</h2>

On the bright side, I love building things, actually that’s why I wanted to become a software engineer. And I love building things even more with a super human buddy by my side to help. My next stage is already underway, and maybe it will bring with it a new form of freedom or maybe it will bring with it a new form of pain. But it’s the only thing that I can think of doing. That is to build things to solve problems for people, and to ask by right of their satisfaction for their payments in return. If employers see no value in my skills, maybe someone out there as a user will see value in the things I build. My current stage is called Kanji Kaku, an app designed and built by me and my super intelligent friends to teach anyone to write Japanese kanji characters. The vision for it comes from my years of studying kanji, and learning from all the great teachers and apps before me. Please try it out if it sounds interesting.
